# Participants — eight archive traders

> **These are planning participants, 2026-10-16.** They are composites written to match
> [the persona](01-persona-archive-trader.md) and to cover the spread of region, device, eyesight
> and goal the site has to serve. Each one ran a scripted session on the live site (log in
> [04-test-plan.md](04-test-plan.md#9-pilot-run--scripted-walkthrough-2026-10-16)). Recruit real
> people to these profiles before treating any finding as confirmed.
>
> The brief describes traders as mostly men aged 40–60. Seven of the eight match that; one is a
> woman, because women trade too and a single-gender panel would hide their needs.

## P1 — Jürgen Becker

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, location | 56, Cologne, Germany (CET) |
| Language | German first; good written English, slower when reading |
| Work | IT systems administrator for a municipal utility |
| Trading since | 1991 (VHS by post); Dime A Dozen member since 2004; uploads his own WDR captures |
| Collection | About 3,800 titles, mostly German TV festival broadcasts; PAL only |
| Setup | Windows 11, 27" 1440p monitor, Firefox, MediaInfo always open |
| Eyesight | Glasses; fine at 100% zoom |
| Tech confidence | Expert |

**Background.** Jürgen recorded Rockpalast and Rock am Ring straight off satellite onto S-VHS,
then onto a Technisat receiver once DVB arrived. He knows the difference between the original WDR
transmission and the later Eins Festival rebroadcast by the logo in the corner. He types in
shorthand: "RATM RaR 96", "FNM Bizarre 97".

**Overlap with The Vault.** Rock am Ring (54 records), Rockpalast (24), Bizarre Festival (22).
He owns RATM at Rock am Ring 1996-05-24 and Faith No More at Bizarre 1997. The Vault holds RATM
RaR 96 as a 24-minute **NTSC** copy (a conversion of a German PAL broadcast, a red flag to him)
and FNM Bizarre 1997 in two copies, 79 and 52 minutes.

**How he judges a recording.** Broadcaster and original vs rebroadcast → PAL native → lineage
(DVB stream, no re-encode) → completeness → logos.

**What he wants from the site.** Confirm in under a minute whether there's anything better than
what he has for his twenty German festival shows.

**Likely behaviour.** Types abbreviations, then the full name. Opens a show, scrolls straight to
the technical details. Looks for lineage, then for MediaInfo-style specs.

**Anticipated pain points.** Abbreviations fail ("RATM" suggests Rammstein); no lineage; no
broadcaster; can't tell the NTSC conversion is a conversion; size covers the whole disc.

> "NTSC for Rock am Ring? Then somebody converted it. Where's the lineage?"

## P2 — Dale Kowalski

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, location | 59, Columbus, Ohio, USA (ET) |
| Language | English |
| Work | Owns a small HVAC business |
| Trading since | 1993; mostly B&P by mail, now Facebook trading groups |
| Collection | About 2,100 titles: every Pearl Jam TV appearance he can find, plus grunge-era late-night TV |
| Setup | iPad Pro in the evenings on the couch; an old Windows laptop for his Excel list |
| Eyesight | Reading glasses; increases text size on the iPad |
| Tech confidence | Moderate: confident in his routine, doesn't explore menus |

**Background.** Dale keeps a 2,100-row Excel sheet with columns he has used since 1998: Date ·
Artist · Venue/Show · Source · Length · Gen · Notes. He compares any new list to it line by line,
with the list on the iPad and the sheet on the laptop.

**Overlap with The Vault.** Pearl Jam (21 records): MTV Unplugged 1992, SNL, Pinkpop 1992 and
2000 (three copies of 2000: 44, 47 and 75 min), VH1 Storytellers 2006 (two copies, both 44 min),
Reading 2006 (four records, two of them excerpts). Letterman (15 records, none Pearl Jam).

**How he judges a recording.** Is it complete? Is it the full broadcast or a clip? What
generation?

**What he wants from the site.** "Show me every Pearl Jam you have, with dates, so I can tick off
my list."

**Likely behaviour.** Scrolls the A–Z list to Pearl Jam and scrolls the cards slowly. Tries to
read dates off the cards. Wants to print or export.

**Anticipated pain points.** Cards show only the year, so two Pinkpop 2000 cards look identical;
faint text in the A–Z list; no list view; no export; must open 21 drawers one by one.

> "Three Pinkpop 2000s and I can't tell which is which without clicking every one."

## P4 — Kenji Morita

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, location | 52, Osaka, Japan (JST) |
| Language | Japanese first; reads English carefully and literally |
| Work | Print production manager |
| Trading since | 1996; trades with UK and European collectors by email |
| Collection | About 2,600 titles: Britpop and UK indie, Japanese TV (WOWOW, Space Shower TV), Summer Sonic and Fuji Rock |
| Setup | MacBook Pro 16" Retina, Safari and Chrome |
| Eyesight | Mild presbyopia; uses Cmd + to zoom |
| Tech confidence | High; pixel-precise by profession |

**Background.** Kenji judges picture quality like a print proof: sharpness, compression blocks,
colour, interlacing combs. He wants to see a still at 100% before he trusts a description.

**Overlap with The Vault.** Japan (30 records), Summer Sonic (9, mostly excerpts), Supergrass (24,
including Liquid Room Tokyo 2004 and the VH2 Live Special 2005).

**How he judges a recording.** Stills at native size → resolution and standard → bitrate →
audio codec (prefers LPCM).

**What he wants from the site.** Decide from the stills whether a Supergrass broadcast is a clean
master or a soft re-encode.

**Likely behaviour.** Opens the screenshot viewer and tries to zoom. Reads every technical label
literally and looks up unfamiliar words.

**Anticipated pain points.** The viewer is capped at the drawer's width (a 768×576 still shown at
671×503 on his 16" screen); no 1:1 or "open original"; "(native)" and `pcm_dvd` are unclear; no
bitrate.

> "I want to see the pixels. A small picture tells me nothing about the encode."

## P5 — Gareth Hughes

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, location | 49, Manchester, UK (GMT/BST) |
| Language | English |
| Work | Data analyst at an insurer |
| Trading since | 1997; Dime and Traders Den; runs a Glastonbury collectors' spreadsheet |
| Collection | About 9,000 rows in Excel: Glastonbury, Reading and Leeds, T in the Park, Jools Holland; hunts BBC HD upgrades of SD sets |
| Setup | Windows desktop with two 24" monitors, Chrome; spreadsheet on one screen, browser on the other |
| Eyesight | Fine |
| Tech confidence | Expert; lives in filters and pivot tables |

**Background.** Gareth replaced most of his 2000s BBC Three SD captures with BBC HD streams after
2008 and keeps both copies with a "superseded" flag. His whole method is comparison.

**Overlap with The Vault.** Glastonbury (71), Reading (64 + 13 Reading and Leeds), T in the Park

(5.65 GB) and a 1080 MKV (2.59 GB).

**How he judges a recording.** HD vs SD → bitrate → audio channels → runtime → logos.

**What he wants from the site.** "Which of your four is the HD one, and is it the full set?"
Then: export the Glastonbury list to compare against his spreadsheet.

**Likely behaviour.** Opens all four copies in turn, writing specs down. Looks for a compare view,
then for CSV.

**Anticipated pain points.** The four cards are identical; resolution never shown; the 1080 TS is
labelled **NTSC**; only one of the four is marked "Proshot"; no compare; no export; Escape wipes
his search while he compares.

> "Four copies, same card, same title. I've opened them all and I still don't know which is HD."

**Overlap with The Vault.** MusiquePlus (12), Much Music Intimate & Interactive (5), Smashing
