Bottom line

On a phone the site works, but it shows a trader one show at a time and loses his place. Every phone narrower than 400px, which includes every standard iPhone and the Galaxy S line, gets one card per screen. An artist opened from the A–Z list starts at the wrong end of its shows. Back leaves the site after any search. With filters on, nearly half the screen is controls.

The show itself is in good shape on a phone. Links open the exact recording, Back closes it in place, sharing hands over a one-line reference, and the empty state names the filter to blame.

Biggest wins, in order: fix the A–Z jump (R1, minutes); two columns on every phone (R2, one line of code: 1 → 4–6 shows per screen); initials that work beside other words (R3); results that start under the bar and above the keyboard (R4); Back that steps back through the site (R5).

1. R1 · An artist opened from the A–Z list starts at the top of its shows

CriticalScore 9.0 = 3 × 3 × 1.0 ÷ 1Confidence 1.0Batch M1 Navigation
→

Tapping Pearl Jam from the list today lands on the last two cards. After: the top, title and count in view.

What changes, exactly

  • On artist select, jump to the top instantly (behavior: 'auto') instead of the smooth scroll that the page swap cancels; or scroll before the list is replaced by results.
  • Files: App.tsx (handleArtistSelect), src/lib/motion.ts (an instant option). Desktop likely has the same bug with a shorter list; check it in the same change.

Why

  • Dale’s artist run (S4) starts on two undated tapes with no title. He would read it as the end of the list, or as all there is.
  • Every A–Z tap on a phone starts at least 5.6 screens down the page, so every artist opened from the list lands somewhere other than the top. Measured: y 12,441 → 6,477, the bottom of a 7,141px page.
  • Confidence 1.0 in Chrome’s engine (Android). On iPhone Safari the same is expected: a smooth scroll cut short by the page shrinking. Worth one tap on a real iPhone.

What changes, exactly

What changes, exactly

What changes, exactly

What works on a phone — keep it

  • Links open the exact recording, and the per-show page previews it in chats. × and Back close it; Back lands at the same scroll position (6,477 → 6,477).
  • Share hands the phone’s share sheet a one-line reference (“Foo Fighters · 2000-01-16 · Big Day Out · SD 576 · 1h 10m”) and the show’s own link.
  • The tab bar: labelled, under the thumb; Search focuses the field and raises the keyboard in one tap.
  • Search understands traders: 24.05.96, 16/01/00, brasil, multishow, channel v, glasto 2009 all work.
  • The empty state names the filters to blame and offers “Show 22 without the filters”.
  • Honest dates: “2001 (date unknown)” in the drawer.
  • Holds at 125% text, with no sideways scrolling at any size tested (312–750px).
  • 16px search field: iOS does not zoom on focus.
  • The list view: 8 dense, readable rows per screen. A good answer for completists today.
  • Speed: first paint 1.9s on slow 4G with a 4× slower CPU; about 700 KB to the first screen.

Limits — what to check on a real phone

This was scripted Chromium with phone emulation (touch, 2× pixels, mobile browser, the visible area after the browser’s bars), not people and not real devices. The persona reactions are predicted. Five things only a real phone can confirm, about two minutes each:

  1. iPhone Safari: tap an artist in the A–Z list. Does it also land at the bottom (R1)?
  2. iPhone Safari: the edge-swipe back after a search. Does it leave the site (R5)?
  3. The real keyboard: how many results show above it after typing “brasil” (R4)?
  4. More filters on the landing page: can you reach “New” at all (R8, R13)?
  5. Pinch-zoom on a still in the viewer: does it zoom (not tested; emulation can’t pinch)?
